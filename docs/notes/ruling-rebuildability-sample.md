# Ruling rebuildability sample (P02)

Read 31 Oct 2026. Switch gate 2 asks: do at least 10 of the 20 most recent ASA rulings on food supplement or health claims describe the ad well enough to rebuild it?

**Answer: yes.** 18 of 20 are rebuildable, 2 partly and none no. The gate counts only "yes"; counting "partly" as well gives 20 of 20, so the answer doesn't change.

## Method

1. Searched the rulings page on asa.org.uk for "food supplement" and "health claims", most recent first, then repeated the search with a date range for older rulings.
2. Kept rulings about health or nutrition claims for foods and food supplements. Excluded: A26-1343103 (dishwasher tablets advertorial), A26-1339455 (stem-cell clinic), G26-1343656 (magnetic bracelet), A25-1317903 (bioresonance test), G26-1334485 (wine, social responsibility), A25-1317840 (nutrition claims for alcoholic drinks) and A25-1293088 (topical magnesium butter).
3. Took the 20 most recent. The 20th place fell between two rulings dated 29 Oct 2025, and only one of them was in scope (A25-1291498).
4. Judged each ruling from its "Ad description" section only, and wrote the judgement down before reading anything else. The test: could a reconstruction carry the claim wording, the medium and any imagery the description relies on?
5. Then read the Issue and Assessment sections to record the CAP rules cited and to check whether the claims the ruling assessed appear in the description ("check" column). This check came after the judgement and did not change it.

Page text is stored under `data/raw/asa-rulings/<ruling-id>.txt` (git-ignored). `docs/source-terms.md` explains why the full text stays out of the repo.

## Findings that affect the plan

- **Every one of the 20 was upheld.** Neither search returned a formal ruling that was not upheld. The negatives in the evaluation protocol (not-upheld rulings in the same topics) will be scarce. P14 should plan to rely on team-written ads that use verbatim register wording, and should search for not-upheld rulings deliberately rather than by date.
- **Many rulings cite rules outside section 15.** Section 12 (medicinal claims, health professional endorsements: 12.1, 12.2, 12.11, 12.18), section 3 (misleading advertising: 3.1, 3.7, 3.9, 3.11), 1.3 (social responsibility) and 1.7 (unreasonable delay) all appear, in 12 of the 20. Only one ruling cites no section 15 rule at all (A24-1261050, rule 3.1 only). Section 12 always appears alongside section 15 here, because the ASA cites both when a food is claimed to treat a disease. The P12 rule pack and the ground truth in P14 need to decide which of these the checker covers. Rule 1.7 concerns the advertiser's conduct and can't be screened from the ad.
- **Descriptions name real people:** a company founder and a television presenter (G26-1339905), an entrepreneur (A24-1261050), a boxer (A25-1291498), named doctors (A26-1325179, A25-1313823, G25-1323444). Under rule 9 a reconstruction removes branding. It should also use a generic presenter or voice rather than any likeness of these people.
- **Video is common.** 7 of 20 include video, usually one person speaking to camera, which a voiceover reconstruction can carry. Most of the rest are image ads with text or web pages, which fit image and page-screenshot reconstructions.
- **The ASA elides text** with "[…]" in most descriptions. A reconstruction carries only the quoted text, so a claim inside an elision can't be rebuilt. The ground truth should be written to match what the description quotes.
- Coverage: keyword search can miss in-scope rulings that use neither search phrase. The sample is the 20 most recent that the two searches found, not a complete census.
