Judge each claim listed below, from a UK ad for a food or food supplement, against the GB nutrition and health claims register, the DHSC list of health claims on hold and the CAP Code rules listed below.
Judge what each claim means to an ordinary consumer, read in the context of the whole ad: its transcript, its on-screen text and its other claims.
Give every claim one verdict: possible_breach, on_hold or no_issue.

A health claim may be used only when an authorised register entry for the same substance says the same thing to the consumer.
A health claim that goes further than its authorised entry, by amplifying, broadening or adding to its effect, is a possible breach.
A health claim that matches a non-authorised register entry, or no authorised entry for its substance, is a possible breach of 15.1.1, and of 15.7 when the product is a food supplement.
A health claim whose substance and effect match an entry on the on-hold list, and no non-authorised register entry, gets on_hold citing that entry; it does not breach 15.1.1.
A general claim about overall health or well-being is a possible breach of 15.2 unless a specific authorised health claim appears in the same ad.
A claim that the product prevents, treats or cures a disease or condition, including naming a condition the product helps with, is a possible breach of 15.6.2 whatever the register says.
Claims about proof, results, speed, purity, strength, popularity, comparisons and endorsements are judged against the other rules listed below; they have no register entry.
A rule about the ad as a whole, such as being recognisable as an ad, is cited on the claim that carries the breach.
The claims overlap: a phrase is listed together with the claims inside it. Judge each on its own words; a phrase that contains a breaching claim is itself a possible breach.
A claim that repeats an authorised entry's wording for a product containing that substance is no_issue unless another rule applies.
Ingredient lists, product names and statements that suggest no effect are no_issue unless a rule applies to them.

For a possible breach, cite every CAP rule it may breach, the register entries that show it (the non-authorised entry it matches, or the authorised entries it goes beyond) and any listed ruling that found a similar claim in breach.
A claim that is on hold and also breaches another rule is a possible breach citing that rule and the on-hold entry.
Give the reason in one or two plain sentences: what the claim says and why it may breach each cited rule.
Never write, shorten or reword a claim in the reason.
Set suggested_register_id to an authorised register entry listed below only when its wording could replace a possible-breach or on-hold health claim about the same substance; otherwise null.
For no_issue, cite nothing, set suggested_register_id to null and keep the reason to a few words.
Cite only the rule numbers and IDs listed below.
The past rulings are earlier ASA decisions on other ads, given to show how the ASA applies the rules; they say nothing about this ad.
